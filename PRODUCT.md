# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences, both evaluating whether to trust José with real data work:

- **Hiring managers for data engineering roles.** They usually scan in a few minutes, between other candidates, on a desktop at work or a phone between meetings. They want evidence of engineering judgment, scale, and ownership. Lists of tools don't tell them that.
- **Consulting clients.** They have a messy data problem: conversions, validation, reporting. They need to believe he can take that problem and deliver a system that holds up.

The job to be done: decide, quickly and with confidence, whether to start a conversation.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for José Picado, a **Data Engineer & Consultant who builds pipelines and validation systems**. He is not a "Workday Consultant". Workday is one domain he has delivered in, not his identity.

The site proves capability through real case studies instead of listing tools. Each project shows the problem, what was built, and a verified metric. If a number isn't verified, it doesn't appear.

Success means a visitor books a call (https://cal.com/jose-picado-uieppc/30min), sends a message (the form or WhatsApp), or downloads the CV. The content is bilingual: **English by default**, with an ES toggle.

## Brand Personality

**Precise, calm, proven.**

The voice is an engineer's confidence: understated, exact, and backed by evidence. It reads as "a builder, not a tool operator". Claims are short and concrete, and each one points to proof. Nothing is oversold, there is no hype vocabulary, and he doesn't pad with adjectives. The emotional goal is quiet trust: the visitor should feel they're looking at someone who checks their work.

## Voice examples

These lines calibrate tone. They are not final copy.

- **Hero headline:** "I build data pipelines, and the systems that prove they're right."
- **Project card:** "**Multi-Client Validation System** · 1M rows across multiple client datasets · Replaced manual spot checks with automated validation, so every load is verified before anyone sees it." *(Tone sample only. "Replaced manual spot checks" is **unverified** and must not ship until José confirms or rewrites it.)*
- **CTA:** "Book a 30-minute call"

The test: short sentences, concrete nouns, and a claim that points to proof. If a line would fit on any portfolio, rewrite it.

## Anti-references

- **Bootstrap portfolios.** Template grids, default navbars, the "Hi, I'm X 👋" hero.
- **AI-slop gradients.** Purple-to-blue washes, gradient text, glowing blobs.
- **Percentage skill bars.** Including any "Python 90%" self-rating meter, ring or star rating.
- **Stock corporate photography.** Handshakes, people pointing at laptops, server-room blue.
- **Workday branding as identity.** No Workday logos, colors or "Workday Consultant" framing in the hero.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the system, not the toolbox.** Every section earns its place by demonstrating built work. Tools appear only as context for a result.
2. **Verified or absent.** Metrics, client names and claims appear only when confirmed. A missing number beats an invented one. Until José clears a client name, it appears only as an industry descriptor (e.g. "a global cybersecurity company").
3. **Practice what you preach.** A site about validation systems must itself be correct: accessible, fast, and free of broken states.
4. **Calm over clever.** Motion and 3D are there to serve comprehension and atmosphere. If an effect draws attention to itself, it gets cut.
5. **One signal at a time.** Each screen has one primary action. Emphasis is scarce, so it means something.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG 2.2 AA** target, with Lighthouse Accessibility ≥ 95.
- **Reduced motion is honored everywhere.** Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, the 3D hero becomes a static fallback and scroll-driven motion is turned off. Low-power devices get the same fallback.
- Full keyboard navigation with visible focus states. Landmarks and a logical heading order.
- Text contrast meets AA on the dark background. The accent is never the only way meaning is conveyed.
- Bilingual ES/EN. The language switch updates `lang` and all visible text.
